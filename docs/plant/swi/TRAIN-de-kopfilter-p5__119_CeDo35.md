## Body text (verbatim, Dutch)

> -5-
> Om aan het einde een hoog waardige pellet (korrel) te krijgen vindt er nog een laatste filtering plaats in het proces, nl de kopfilter. De filters in de piston onder en boven kunnen vervangen worden.
>
> De filters bestaan uit 2 steunzeven en het filter

**English gloss:** "-5- To obtain a high-quality pellet (korrel) at the end, a final filtering takes place in the process, namely the **kopfilter (head filter)**. The filters in the **piston** — **lower and upper** — can be replaced. The filters consist of **2 support screens (steunzeven) and the filter** (i.e. a screen pack: filter mesh sandwiched between two support screens)."

## Notes / relevance to open questions — HITS

- **Q15 / filtering stages — CLARIFIES two distinct filters.** The extruder train has TWO melt-filtration stages:
  1. **Laserfilter** (rotary self-cleaning disc filter, on the barrel ~zone 3) — 112_CeDo32.
  2. **Kopfilter** (head/screen-pack filter at the die head, downstream) — this slide. It is a **piston-type screen changer with replaceable upper & lower filter packs**, each pack = **2 support screens (steunzeven) + 1 filter mesh**. This is the FINAL filtration to guarantee a high-quality pellet.
  So on the plant map, melt path = Extruder + laserfilter (+vacuum) → **Kopfilter (piston screen-pack, upper/lower, replaceable)** → smeltslang (melt hose) → pelletizer/dewatering. Matches "Kopfilter & smeltslang" box in De Flow (116_CeDo7). Evidence: "de kopfilter. De filters in de piston onder en boven kunnen vervangen worden."
- **Operations detail (game-relevant):** the upper/lower piston filters are **operator-replaceable** consumables (screen changes) — a maintenance action. When they clog, melt pressure rises. Reference video preserved: youtube.com/watch?v=HtjRVhxv1yc.
- No numeric setpoints (mesh size, pressure trip) on this slide.
