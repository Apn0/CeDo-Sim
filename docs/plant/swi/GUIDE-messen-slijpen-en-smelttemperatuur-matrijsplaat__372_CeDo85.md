## Section 1 — "Messen slijpen doe je ook met plezier" (grinding knives — do it with pleasure)

Bullets (verbatim NL):
- "een nieuw mes is plat" (a new knife is flat)
- "de snijkant is recht" (the cutting edge is straight)
- "een gebruikt blad is gegolfd/getand" (a used blade is wavy/serrated)
- "de snijkant is niet meer recht" (the cutting edge is no longer straight)

**"Resultaat: Het mes laat krassen achter[,] beschadigingen op de matrijsplaat"** (Result: the knife leaves scratches behind — damage on the die plate.)

## Section 2 — "Smelt temperatuur" (melt temperature — diagnose via strand appearance at die)

Three photos of polymer strands emerging from the die/matrijsplaat, each captioned:

| Photo | Caption (NL) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Left (grey, stubby broken strands) | **"te koud" / "niet gesmolten materiaal"** | too cold / not-melted material — strands short, crumbly, incomplete |
| Middle (smooth flowing purple strands) | **"goed gehard" / "vloeiende smelt"** | correctly conditioned / freely flowing melt — the GOOD state |
| Right (long thin drooping purple strands, some scorching) | **"te heet" / "verbrande smelt"** | too hot / burnt melt — strands over-long, thin, degraded |

## Q&A hits
- **Q15 (temp zones / why zone3=175):** supports melt-temperature diagnosis logic — operator judges melt temp visually at the die (too cold = unmelted, good = flowing, too hot = burnt). No numeric zone values on this page, but this is the qualitative reference behind temperature-zone tuning.
- **Q20 (slow-running remedies):** worn/wavy knife → scratches/damage on die plate → poor pellet cut → potential slow-running; remedy = regrind knives flat/straight. And melt-temp diagnosis: adjust heat if strands read too cold or too hot.
